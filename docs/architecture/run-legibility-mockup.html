Pressures on the Indian Rupee, Nov 2025 – Nov 2026

The rupee depreciated 6.4% against the dollar (83.1 → 88.4) in two sharp legs — April, after the tariff announcement, and September, on the oil spike. Ranked by contribution:

  • Oil import costs — the largest driver: ~85% imported crude, a $10/bbl move ≈ $1.6bn/month on the bill.
  • Foreign portfolio flows — $14bn of FPI outflows in H1; the rate differential is the main lever.
  • Fed policy — 100 bp of 2026 cuts now priced; a narrower differential is the main upside risk for INR.
  • Trade deficit — $283bn annualised, structurally persistent but slow-moving.
  • RBI intervention — ≈$42bn net spot sales; reserves down from $705bn to ~$668bn (sources differ on the exact drawdown — flagged).

Peer comparison. INR's −6.4% sits between the yen (−5%) and the won (−8%) — a middle-of-the-pack year for Asian ex-US; the rupee's lower volatility reflects the intervention, at a reserve cost.

Outlook (6 months). Range-bound 87–90 with a mild depreciation bias; the swing factor is the Fed path — faster cuts narrow the differential and could pull INR back toward 86. Oil above $85/bbl is the main downside risk.
